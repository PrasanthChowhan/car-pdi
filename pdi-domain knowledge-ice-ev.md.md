# New Car PDI – Forensic Checklist (ICE + EV Anti‑Scam Version)

Treat this PDI like a forensic inspection: assume the dealer will cut corners unless you verify every single detail yourself.
Refuse delivery if anything serious is wrong.

Use this document on your phone or print it and tick items as you go.
This checklist works for both ICE (petrol/diesel/CNG) and EVs, with extra sections for each.

---

## 0. Absolute Deal-Breakers (Dealer Red Flags)

**Before you even start the physical inspection, watch out for these massive red flags from the dealership. If you experience these, strongly consider walking away. NEVER buy a car from a dealer exhibiting these behaviors.**

- **Refusing the PDI:** The biggest red flag. If the dealer refuses to let you perform a PDI, denies access to the stockyard, or claims "company policy" forbids it, **walk away**. They are hiding something (transit damage, demo car, old stock). You are fully entitled to inspect before registration.
- **Demanding Full Payment Before PDI:** Never pay the full amount before you have personally inspected the car and are satisfied. Only the booking amount should be paid beforehand. Dealers create artificial urgency ("pay now to unlock the car/start registration"); do not fall for it.
- **Forcing Evening/Dark Inspections:** The dealer insists you do the PDI in the evening, at night, or in a dimly lit basement/showroom. This is a classic tactic to hide paint defects, repaints, and panel misalignments. **Insist on daytime, open natural light.**
- **"Locked" Features or Restricted Access:** The dealer claims the infotainment system is "locked," or you cannot test the sunroof, or they block you from using an OBD scanner. They may be hiding underlying error codes or faulty electronics.
- **Evasive About the VIN:** The dealer hesitates or refuses to share the Vehicle Identification Number (VIN) or engine number before you arrive. They may be trying to slip you a much older manufactured car.
- **Refusing Third-Party Mechanics:** If you want to bring a trusted mechanic for a second opinion and the dealer prohibits it, it's a huge warning sign.
- **Rushing You:** Creating high-pressure situations to force a quick sign-off and blind payment. Take your time. Your peace of mind is worth more than a small discount or a rushed delivery.

## 1. Mindset and Preparation

**Goal:** Protect your hard‑earned money by catching scams and defects *before* the car becomes legally yours.

- Visit the **stockyard before registration** if possible; once the car is registered in your name, your bargaining power drops sharply.
- Insist on **daytime inspection in open natural light**; showroom lighting hides dents, repaints, and panel gap issues.
- Bring:
  - Trusted friend/relative (second pair of eyes)
  - Torch/flashlight
  - Tyre pressure gauge
  - OBD scanner (if you have one)
  - Phone for photos and video
- Be polite but firm:
  - You are **not obligated** to accept delivery if there are major defects, wrong documents, or bait‑and‑switch on variant/accessories.
  - Do **not** sign or pay the final amount until everything checks out.

## 2. Documents, Billing, and Money Scams (Common for ICE + EV)

**Focus here first – paperwork scams are common and harder to fix later.**

### 2.1 Price and Charges

- Compare **ex‑showroom price** on the invoice with:
  - Manufacturer’s website
  - Official price lists
- Verify **RTO tax, registration fees, and road tax** against government/official sources.
- Check **insurance**:
  - IDV (Insured Declared Value)
  - Coverage type (comprehensive/third‑party)
  - Add‑ons (zero‑dep, RTI, engine cover, etc.)
  - NCB (no‑claim bonus) if applicable
- Look for **illegal or vague charges**:
  - "Handling", "logistics", "miscellaneous", "value‑added" without clear explanation
  - Ask for a breakup or remove them if they’re not legitimate.

### 2.2 Identity and Details on All Documents

For each document below, confirm **every detail is correct**:

- **Invoice**
- **Insurance policy**
- **Registration / temporary registration**
- **Form 22 (road‑worthiness certificate)**
- **Sales certificate**

Check:

- Your **name** (spelling) and **address**
- **Model, variant, fuel type, transmission, and colour**
- **VIN (chassis number)**
- **Engine number** (for ICE) or **motor ID** (for EV, if listed)

> All numbers on paper must match the physical car exactly.

### 2.3 Manufacturing Date and Stock Age

- Decode **VIN** or check **Form 22** for manufacturing month and year.
- Be extra strict if buying in **Q1** – dealers sometimes push 12–18‑month‑old stock as "new".
- If the car is much older than what was promised, demand:
  - A fresh car
  - Or a heavy discount documented on the invoice.

### 2.4 Must‑Have Documents Before You Leave

Tick these before accepting delivery:

- [ ] Original **invoice**
- [ ] **Sales certificate**
- [ ] **Insurance policy** (with correct details)
- [ ] **RC** or **temporary registration**
- [ ] **PUC certificate** (where applicable – usually after a certain time for new cars in some states)
- [ ] **Owner’s manual**
- [ ] **Service booklet** (stamped by dealer)
- [ ] **Standard vehicle warranty** papers
- [ ] **Extended warranty** and **RSA** documents (if purchased)
- [ ] Separate warranties for:
  - [ ] Battery (especially for EVs)
  - [ ] Tyres
  - [ ] Audio/infotainment
  - [ ] CNG kit (if applicable)

> If anything looks off, stop the process. Do not sign, do not pay the final remaining amount.

## 3. Vehicle Identity and Odometer (Common)

**Goal:** Avoid old stock or a test‑drive car masquerading as new.

### 3.1 Variant and Feature Verification

- Check the **brochure / official website** and your **booking form**.
- Confirm on the actual car:
  - Engine type (ICE: petrol/diesel/CNG, turbo vs non‑turbo; EV: motor power/drive type)
  - Transmission type (manual/automatic, CVT/DCT/AT, or single‑speed EV drive)
  - Safety features (airbags count, ABS, ESC, etc.)
  - Infotainment screen size and type
  - Wheel type (alloy vs steel) and size
  - Any other promised features (sunroof, cruise control, LED lights, etc.)

### 3.2 Odometer Reading

- **Ideal:** under **50 km**
- **Acceptable:** up to **~100 km** with a clear explanation (yard movement, transport)
- **Red flag:**
  - More than **100 km** *and/or* visible signs of usage:
    - Worn pedals
    - Scuffed driver seat bolsters
    - Stone chips on front bumper/bonnet
    - Tyres showing wear

If you suspect it’s a test‑drive/display car:

- Ask directly.
- If not clearly explained and compensated, **reject the car**.

### 3.3 Record Keeping

- Take clear **photos** of:
  - VIN (usually on door frame or engine bay)
  - Engine number or motor ID
  - Odometer reading
  - Tyre manufacturing dates (sidewall code)
- Save these photos with the date as your personal record.

## 4. Exterior Body, Paint, and Panel Gaps (Common)

**Assume they may have repaired transit damage and hope you won’t notice.**

### 4.1 Inspection Conditions

- Inspect the car **outdoors in daylight**.
- Walk around the car slowly.
- Look at panels from different angles (front, side, rear, low and high viewpoints).

### 4.2 What to Look For on Paint and Panels

- Scratches, dents, dings, ripples
- Colour shade differences between neighbouring panels
- Orange‑peel texture or roughness
- Overspray on rubber/plastic parts
- Uneven gloss, dull spots, or cloudiness

> These can indicate repainting or poor repair.

### 4.3 High‑Risk Areas

Check carefully around:

- Door edges
- Bumper corners (front and rear)
- Roof edges
- ORVM housings (mirror covers)
- Door handles

### 4.4 Panel Gaps and Operation

- Doors, bonnet, and boot/tailgate:
  - Gaps must be **even and consistent** left to right.
  - No panel sticking out or sunk in.
- Open and close each door, bonnet, and boot/tailgate:
  - No grinding or loud metallic noises
  - Should close with a solid, consistent thud
  - Rubber beadings must be intact, soft, and properly seated.

### 4.5 Transit Damage and Repaints

If you see signs of repainting or major dents:

- Ask explicitly: "Has this car had any transit damage or body repair?"
- Demand **written acknowledgement** and a **rectification plan** on dealer letterhead.
- For serious damage or repaint on major panels:
  - Prefer **rejecting this unit** and asking for a different car.

## 5. Tyres, Wheels, and Brakes (Common)

**Dealers sometimes swap tyres/wheels or deliver old stock rubber.**

### 5.1 Tyre Brand, Size, and Type

- Compare tyre details with official spec:
  - Brand
  - Size (e.g., 195/60 R16)
  - Type (tubeless, run‑flat, etc.)

### 5.2 Tyre Age and Condition

- Check **manufacturing week/year** on the sidewall (e.g., 2425 = week 24, year 2025).
  - All four should be similar and reasonably recent.
- Inspect each tyre for:
  - Cuts
  - Bubbles
  - Sidewall damage
  - Uneven wear
  - Embedded nails or sharp objects
- Tread should clearly look **new**.

### 5.3 Wheels and Spare Wheel

- Inspect wheel rims/alloys:
  - No curb rash or scratches
  - No bends or cracks
- Spare wheel:
  - Present
  - Correct size (as per manufacturer strategy – full‑size or space saver)
  - Tread looks unused
  - Properly mounted and accessible
- Tool kit:
  - Jack
  - Spanner
  - Wheel lock key (if applicable)
  - Towing hook

### 5.4 Tyre Pressure and Brakes

- Ask dealer to set **correct tyre pressure**; over‑inflation is common and affects ride and braking.
- On test drive, check **brake feel**:
  - Pedal should feel firm and progressive, not spongy
  - Car must brake in a straight line with no pulling or vibration

## 6. Glass, Lights, and External Fittings (Common)

**Small cracks or misfitted parts can become expensive later.**

### 6.1 Glass

- Windscreen
- Front and rear door windows
- Quarter glass
- Rear windshield
- Sunroof (if equipped)

Check for:

- Chips
- Cracks
- Deep scratches
- Distortion in view

### 6.2 Lights

Inspect lamp units for:

- Moisture or fogging inside
- Cracks or broken clips
- Poor sealing or gaps around edges

Test functions:

- Headlights high/low beam
- DRLs
- Front and rear indicators
- Brake lights
- Reverse lights
- Fog/auxiliary lights
- Hazard lights

### 6.3 External Fittings

- Plastic cladding and trims
- Roof rails
- ORVMs (mirror housings and mirrors)

Check for:

- Proper fitment (flush with body)
- No cracks or loose parts

## 7. Engine Bay, Fluids, and Underbody (ICE‑Specific)

**Only for ICE vehicles – skip this section for pure EVs.**

### 7.1 Overall Condition

- Open the bonnet.
- Engine bay should be:
  - Clean
  - Free of oil/coolant stains
  - Free of excessive dust/mud for a "new" vehicle

### 7.2 Fluids

Check:

- Engine oil:
  - Level at or near correct mark on dipstick
  - Colour consistent for a new engine
- Coolant:
  - Level in reservoir
  - No crusted leaks around hoses
- Brake fluid:
  - Level near "MAX" mark
- Windshield washer fluid:
  - Present and topped up

### 7.3 Fuel and Exhaust

- Fuel area:
  - No smell of raw petrol/diesel around car or inside cabin
  - No visible leaks around fuel lines/tank zone
- Exhaust:
  - Pipe properly aligned, not bent or hanging low
  - No excessive smoke under moderate revs

### 7.4 Wiring and Components

Look for:

- Tangled or cut wires
- Different‑coloured plastic parts or hoses suggesting replacements
- Non‑OEM clamps, tape, or messy repairs

### 7.5 Start and Short Drive Check (ICE)

- Start engine:
  - Should start quickly and smoothly
  - Idle should be stable
  - No metallic knocking or abnormal vibrations
- After short drive:
  - Check under bonnet and underbody for fresh leaks or drips

## 8. EV Drive Unit, Battery, and High‑Voltage Systems (EV‑Specific)

**Only for EVs – skip this section for pure ICE vehicles.**

### 8.1 High‑Voltage Battery Health and Warnings

- Check any available **battery health/SoH** screen or diagnostic report.
- Confirm **state of charge (SOC)** is reasonable and there are **no battery or HV system warning lights**.
- Ask the dealer if the car is on the **latest battery/BMS software calibration**.

### 8.2 Charging Ports and Cables

- Inspect the **charging port**:
  - Lid opens/closes smoothly
  - Rubber seals intact
  - Port properly aligned with body
- Verify all **included charging equipment**:
  - Portable charging cable
  - Home wallbox (if part of package)
  - Adapters (Type 2, CCS, etc., if promised)
- Check cables and plugs for:
  - No cuts or damage
  - Correct rating/ampere markings

### 8.3 Live Charging Demo

- Ask for a **live charging demonstration**:
  - Plug the car into a charger
  - Confirm charging starts and status shows correctly on car screen
  - No intermittent disconnects or error messages
  - See how charging status/range updates over a few minutes

### 8.4 Range, Energy Use, and Regenerative Braking

- Compare **displayed range** with official claimed range for that SOC (small difference is normal).
- On short test drive:
  - Observe how the **estimated range changes** over 5–10 km
  - Avoid big, erratic drops that don’t match your driving style
- Test **regenerative braking** levels:
  - Switch between available regen modes
  - Confirm behaviour is smooth and predictable, not jerky or inconsistent

### 8.5 Thermal Management

- Check that **battery thermal management** (cooling/heating) appears to function when needed (often visible as fans/AC behaviour or through EV menu).
- In hot weather, watch how much **range drops when AC is on**;
  - Some drop is normal
  - Wild swings might indicate calibration issues

### 8.6 EV Warranties and Service Plans

- Confirm **battery warranty** (often 8 years / X km) is clearly documented.
- Check any **EV‑specific service plan or extended warranty** is mentioned in writing.

## 9. Interior, Upholstery, and Cabin Quality (Common)

**Interior defects and water damage can be very annoying later.**

### 9.1 General Condition

Inspect:

- Seats (front and rear)
- Door pads
- Roof liner
- Dashboard and center console
- Armrests
- Carpets

Look for:

- Stains
- Torn fabric or leather
- Loose stitching
- Broken plastics or sharp edges

### 9.2 Floor and Water Damage

- Lift floor mats.
- Check underneath for:
  - Dampness
  - Water stains
  - Mud marks

These can indicate storage in flooded yards or leaks.

### 9.3 Seat and Belt Operation

- Test all seat adjustments (manual/electric) for:
  - Fore‑aft movement
  - Recline
  - Height and lumbar (if available)
- Seat belts (all seats):
  - Retract smoothly
  - Lock properly when pulled sharply

### 9.4 Storage and Smell

- Glovebox:
  - Opens/closes properly
  - Cooled function (if equipped) works
- All storage cubbies and armrests:
  - Open and lock properly
- Smell:
  - No foul odour from AC vents
  - No strong mouldy or chemical smell in cabin

## 10. Electronics, Infotainment, and Controls (Common, with EV Screens)

**Dealers sometimes deliver cars with half‑working electronics and hope you’ll "fix under warranty."**

### 10.1 Instrument Cluster and Warning Lights

- With ignition/power on, watch the cluster:
  - All warning lights should come on briefly.
  - After start, **no critical warning lights** (engine for ICE, motor/battery for EV, ABS, airbag, battery, etc.) should stay on.

### 10.2 AC and Climate Control

- AC performance:
  - Cooling must be strong and quick.
  - All blower speeds work.
  - All modes (face, feet, defogger) work.
  - No unusual noises from compressor or blower.

### 10.3 Infotainment and Connectivity

- Touchscreen:
  - Responsive, no heavy lag or random reboots.
- Speakers:
  - All channels work (front/rear, left/right).
- Connectivity:
  - Bluetooth pairing
  - Android Auto / Apple CarPlay
- Cameras and sensors:
  - Reverse camera and/or 360° camera display clearly.
  - Parking sensors beep at appropriate distances.

### 10.4 EV‑Specific Screens (for EVs)

- Battery and range display:
  - Shows realistic SOC (%) and range.
- Energy consumption screen:
  - Updates as you drive.
- Charge scheduling / limit settings:
  - Works as expected.

### 10.5 Switches and Controls

Test:

- Power windows (including auto up/down where available)
- Central locking and child lock
- ORVM adjust and fold
- Steering‑mounted controls (volume, call, cruise, etc.)
- All interior lights (map lights, cabin lamp, boot lamp)

### 10.6 Charging Points and Wipers

- Charging points:
  - USB ports
  - 12V socket
  - Wireless charger (if available)

- Wipers:
  - Front and rear wipers operate smoothly.
  - Washer spray works on both.
  - Intermittent and fast modes function.

### 10.7 Connected‑Car / EV App (for EVs)

- Pair your phone with the official app.
- Confirm you can see:
  - Lock/unlock status
  - Battery %, range
  - Charging status and history
  - Remote AC or pre‑conditioning (if supported)

### 10.8 OBD Scan (Optional)

- If you have an OBD scanner:
  - Run a quick scan for stored error codes.
  - Insist any issues be resolved before delivery.

## 11. Mechanical Feel and Short Road Test (Common, with EV Notes)

**Even a brand‑new car can have alignment, brake, or suspension issues.**

### 11.1 Test Drive Setup

- Take a test drive with dealer representative.
- Try to drive on:
  - City roads
  - Slightly higher‑speed roads (where possible)

### 11.2 Engine / Motor and Ignition

- ICE:
  - Car starts smoothly every time.
  - Engine responds without hesitation or jerks.
- EV:
  - Car powers on without errors.
  - Motor delivers smooth, linear acceleration.

### 11.3 Steering

- On a straight, level road at ~60 km/h:
  - Car should track straight with a light grip.
  - No pull to left or right.
- Turn steering full left and right at low speed:
  - No grinding or loud noises.
  - Steering returns to center properly.

### 11.4 Brakes

- Pedal feel:
  - Firm and progressive.
- Performance:
  - Car brakes straight, without pulling.
  - No strong vibration or shudder.

### 11.5 Suspension and Noise

- Drive over small bumps or rough patches:
  - No rattles, squeaks, or loud thuds.
  - Car should feel tight and well‑screwed together.

### 11.6 Automatics / Single‑Speed EVs

- ICE automatics:
  - Gear changes smooth without excessive head‑nod.
  - Manual mode / paddles respond correctly.
- EVs:
  - No abnormal whine or clunk from drive unit.
  - Creeping behaviour in D/R is predictable and smooth.

> Any pull, vibration, or strange noise is a **fix‑before‑delivery issue**, not "we’ll see at first service".

## 12. Accessories, Freebies, and Promised Add‑ons (Common)

**This is where many "small scams" happen.**

### 12.1 Promised Accessories

- Check booking form and invoice.
- For each promised item, confirm:
  - Installed
  - Correct quality and brand

Common items:

- Floor mats
- Mud flaps
- Seat covers
- Chrome packs or body kits
- Upgraded infotainment/audio
- Dashcam
- For EVs: home charger, cable hooks, dedicated EV parking markings (if promised)

### 12.2 Downgrades and Substitutions

- Make sure there is **no accessory downgrade** unless you agreed:
  - Cheaper aftermarket audio instead of OEM
  - Older dashcam model
  - Non‑OEM seat covers passed off as "company fitted"
- If any change is acceptable to you:
  - Get it **written** and adjust price accordingly.

### 12.3 Tools, Safety Items, and Keys

Confirm presence and condition of:

- Tool kit, jack, towing hook
- First‑aid kit
- Hazard/warning triangle
- Tyre puncture kit or inflator (if provided instead of spare)

Keys:

- Number of keys (all remote keys, valet key if applicable)
- Each key works for lock/unlock and start or EV power‑on.

## 13. Final Delivery, Documentation of Defects, and When to Refuse (Common)

### 13.1 Document Every Issue

- Create a **written list** of all defects or pending items.
- Get it on **dealer letterhead**, signed and stamped.
- Each item should have:
  - Description
  - Agreed solution (repair/replace)
  - Clear deadline (e.g., "scratch on left rear door to be repainted by DD/MM/YYYY").

### 13.2 Use Photos and Video

- Take **photos/videos** of any issue.
- Back them up (cloud or another device).
- These help if you need to escalate to manufacturer or consumer forum.

### 13.3 When to Flatly Refuse Delivery

Refuse delivery (or demand a replacement car) if you find:

- Structural damage or obvious repaint of major panels.
- Serious mechanical or electrical faults (ICE or EV).
- Odometer suggesting test‑drive use (>100 km) with clear wear signs.
- Wrong variant or bait‑and‑switch on features.
- For EVs: major battery/charging errors or repeated HV warnings.

### 13.4 Final Payment and Signing

- Only make final payment **after**:
  - All critical issues are resolved
  - Or formally committed in writing
- Do not let the dealer pressure you with:
  - "Sir, everything is minor, just sign"
  - "We’ll fix it at first service"

## 15. How to Use This at the Showroom (Short Checklist Mode)

1. **Start with documents** – price, VIN, engine/motor ID, manufacturing date, insurance, and warranties.
2. **Walk around the car outside** – body, paint, gaps, glass, tyres, and wheels.
3. **Sit inside** – upholstery, smell, seats, belts, storage.
4. **Power on** – cluster, warning lights, AC, infotainment, switches.
5. **Open bonnet** – ICE: fluids, leaks, wiring, fuel/exhaust. EV: basic visual check only.
6. **EV‑specific** – charging demo, range display, regen behaviour, EV app pairing, battery/HV warnings.
7. **Test drive** – steering, brakes, suspension, engine or motor response.
8. **Check accessories and tools** – promised add‑ons, home charger (for EV), tool kit, keys.
9. **Write down and photograph any issue** – get a signed commitment.
10. **If big problems appear, don’t take delivery** – ask for another car or walk away.

> Your job isn’t to be a car expert. Your job is to be slow, careful, and uncompromising when something serious is wrong.
